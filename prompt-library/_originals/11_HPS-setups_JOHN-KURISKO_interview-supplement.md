Based on the source provided, John Kurisko details three specific scalping setups that rely on his "Quad Rotation" indicator—a combination of four stochastic bands (9-3, 14-3, 44-4, and 60-10) used to identify momentum shifts and market pivots.

## 1. The Super Signal (The "Holy Grail" Quad Divergence)
This is considered the highest probability setup, working approximately 95% of the time when all conditions are met. It combines a Quad Rotation (all timeframes aligned) with a Price Divergence.
* What to Look For:
   * Stage 1: All four stochastic bands must drop under the 20 line (oversold).
   * Stage 2: The price makes a small bounce, causing the 9-3 stochastic to rise slightly.
   * Stage 3: The price drops back down to a level equal to or lower than the first stage, while the 9-3 stochastic stays higher than its previous low (this is the bullish divergence).
* Entry Signal: Enter the trade with a "visual confirmation" the moment the 9-3 stochastic turns back up above the 20 line, activating the divergence signal.
* Exit/Stop-Loss: Place your stop-loss one to two ticks under the low candle of the pattern. Because this setup involves larger timeframes converging, it often results in a "big long trade" rather than a tiny scalp.

## 2. The 20/20 Bull Flag
This is a continuation setup used when the market is in a strong, aggressive uptrend.
* What to Look For:
   * The "Flagpole": Price moves aggressively off the 20 EMA with a steeper-than-average move.
   * Embedded Strength: The 60-10 stochastic (representing the 5-minute trend) must stay "embedded" above 80 (preferably 90), indicating the trend is very strong.
   * The Pullback: The price pulls back toward the 20 EMA while the 9-3 stochastic rotates all the way down to the 20 line.
* Entry Signal: Buy exactly when the 9-3 stochastic hits the 20 line while the price is holding above the 20 EMA. This is considered the "weakest point" of the pullback before the trend resumes.
* Exit/Stop-Loss: While specific exit ticks aren't defined for this scalp, the goal is to capture the next "push up" following the 20 EMA bounce, typically yielding 3 to 5 points.

## 3. The Bear Flag (Embedded Stochastic Rotation)
This setup is the opposite of the bull flag and is used to trade effectively in downtrending markets to avoid getting "stuck" in counter-trend trades.
* What to Look For:
   * Market Context: The price should be trending downward, typically underneath the 200-period moving average.
   * Embedded Weakness: The 60-10 stochastic is "stuck" or "banging around the bottom" underneath the 20-30 level, showing no upward momentum.
* Entry Signal: Sell (short) every time the fast 9-3 stochastic rotates back up above the 80 line. This identifies a "real" bear flag based on internal momentum rather than just visual appearance.
* Exit/Stop-Loss: Take the trade off as soon as the 9-3 stochastic completes its fast rotation back down. This "must take it off" rule prevents you from being caught if the downtrend finally ends.

## Summary of Tools to Use
* Moving Averages: 20 EMA, 50 EMA, and 200 EMA.
* Stochastics (The Quad): Four bands set to 9-3, 14-3, 44-4, and 60-10.  [PDF: 40-4]
* Other Tools: VWAP (Volume Weighted Average Price) and horizontal support/resistance lines.
