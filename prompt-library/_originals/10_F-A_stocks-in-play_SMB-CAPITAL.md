# F-A — "Stocks in Play" selection framework (SMB Capital)
Source: owner-supplied summary of SMB Capital's public framework (YouTube: @smbcapital). Adapted version: plan §8 F-A + strategy S3 (§7).

Filter #1: Reason (What's Changed?)
The first question you must ask is, "Why should this stock move today?" A "Reason" implies that new information has entered the market, forcing participants to revalue the company.
* Good Reasons: Earnings reports, guidance changes, FDA news, M&A headlines, lawsuits, or breaking a multi-month resistance level.
* Weak Reasons: "It looks interesting," "It moved yesterday," "Someone mentioned it," or "I always trade this stock."
* The One-Sentence Test: If you cannot clearly state, "Stock XYZ is in play today because [Reason]," you do not have a trade yet.

Filter #2: Participation (Who Cares?)
While news provides a reason to move, volume confirms if other traders actually care.
* Key Markers:
   * Gaps: Look for stocks gapping up or down at least 3%.
   * Liquidity: The stock should typically trade over 2 million shares a day.
   * Range: It should have an Average True Range (ATR) of over $1.00.
   * Pre-Market Volume: A stock is highly significant if it has already traded 20%, 50%, or even 100% of its average daily volume before the market opens.
* Avoid "Polka Dot Charts": These are charts with no real order flow or thin pre-market volume; they often lead to random, choppy price action.

Filter #3: Structure (Where is the Battlefield?)
Structure refers to a clean chart level or setup that provides a "battlefield" for your decisions.
* Higher Time Frames: Before looking at a 1-minute chart, you must validate the Daily, Hourly, and 15-minute charts.
* Identifying Levels: Is the stock clearing a 52-week high, breaking out of a multi-month base, or failing at major resistance?
* Avoid "No Man's Land": Do not trade stocks gapping into the "M-word"—Mid-range—where price action is messy and levels are unclear.

Good Stock vs Bad Stock
* The Good Stock: Has a fresh catalyst, strong pre-market volume (e.g., 25% of daily average), and is breaking a major consolidation level in a strong sector.
* The Bad Stock: Lacks fresh news, has weak volume, and its major levels are far away.
* The Trap: A "bad stock" can sometimes have a "better-looking" technical pattern than a "good stock." However, because there is no participation or reason, the volume will dry up and the pattern will likely fail.

The Morning Scanner Checklist
1. Run a Gap Scanner: Search for stocks up/down >3%, with >2M average volume and >$1 ATR.
2. Read the News: Match the scanner results to specific catalysts like earnings or FDA news.
3. Prioritize Earnings: These provide the cleanest "new information" to the market.
4. Check Relative Volume: Look for at least 10% of daily volume in the pre-market or 30% within the first 10 minutes of the open.
5. Validate Higher Time Frames: Ensure there is a clean level on the daily or hourly chart.
6. Mid-Day Re-Scan: Around 11:00 AM–12:00 PM, look for stocks that have already exceeded their full-day average volume or ATR.

How to Build Your Focus List
* Cap Your List: Beginners should watch a maximum of three names. Watching more leads to diluted focus and missed entries.
* Prioritize Your Playbook: Even if a stock is in play, if it doesn't match a setup you understand, you must pass.
* Sector Strength: Favor the strongest stocks in strong sectors for long trades, and the weakest stocks in weak sectors for shorts.
* Use Alerts: If a stock is not ready yet, set an alert and stop watching it until the market tells you it is time to re-engage.
